# Bounded execution recovery

Ordinary tooling failure is not a permanent project ban. This method applies inside an authorized unit;
it does not waive user/target prohibitions, privacy, real account rights or mandatory behavior checks.

## Diagnose, repair, continue

- Stop the failing operation, retain safe evidence and distinguish product baseline failure from tool failure.
  Record cause, actual attempts/consumption, partial effects and unknowns; never infer zero traffic or stopped
  processes from a returned tool call. Reconcile ambiguous external mutations before another write.
- The Worker resolves ordinary engineering problems. Report forge-client or shared execution-method faults
  promptly through the Coordinator to the Supervisor; the Supervisor fixes them on the active failure-event
  turn, not at the next heartbeat. Do not send credentials, environment dumps or private report bodies.
- Within existing authority, diagnose and correct paths, arguments, encoding, request format, timeouts and
  task-owned execution helpers. Use bounded causal retries, not account/endpoint shopping, limit evasion or
  repeated unchanged calls. Count prior repairs: after three failed repairs of the same fault stop and notify
  the user with the exact blocker, affected scope and minimum needed action. Notify sooner when unable to
  proceed or human access/identity/credentials are needed; do not keep writing status-only corrections.
- No fresh user approval is needed for each mechanical correction. Real user stops, safety incidents,
  protected/unknown writes, exhausted repair allowance or unavailable rights are not mechanical exceptions.
  Known ordinary historical helper failures can be reassessed under this policy; history is never erased.

## Complete missing setup and validation

Give one complete setup + affected validation + necessary repair + publication unit, not a chain of
metadata-only investigations. Reuse established successes and source provenance. Necessary dependencies
may be supplied inside named task-owned directories/caches/build outputs using whole-unit estimates,
ordinary command timeouts and capacity checks before large supply/build steps. Hard filesystem quotas,
exact transfer/disk accounting and precomputed dependency totals are not prerequisites for installation,
builds or tests; do not provision quota infrastructure or block work solely because it is unavailable.
Estimates guide planning, not automatic stop gates. Check capacity at natural stage boundaries; if growth
materially exceeds the estimate, reduce concurrency/scope or adjust the plan within authority. Stop affected
writes on ENOSPC or actual capacity danger; retain original records. Monitoring is not hard enforcement.
Keep real platform limits, explicit user-imposed ceilings, prior consumption and shared/production boundaries;
no blind full install, shared/global toolchain mutation or unbounded retries.

Preserve spent budgets, failures and original deadlines. Never extend an expired phase retrospectively.
The authorized Supervisor may allocate a realistic explicit missing-work/recovery phase for the existing
mandate, with cumulative accounting and a new prospective deadline; this is not a reset of prior consumption,
a new audit, revival of paused sources or bypass of a true prohibition. Exclude unresolved risky resources
while independent safe units continue. Do not switch executors merely to launder failure history.
